## Where the question comes from

J. J. Andrews and M. L. Curtis posed it in *Free groups and handlebodies*,
Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. **16** (1965). The setting was topological, which is
worth knowing: the moves correspond to handle operations, and the conjecture is
one of several places where a question about presentations is really a question
about four-dimensional topology.

## The candidate counterexamples

| Family | Form | Why it is watched |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Akbulut–Kirby** | `x^n = y^(n+1)` together with `x y x = y x y` | The standard test family, and the one every new search is pointed at first |
| **Miller–Schupp** | a two-generator family with an exponent sum condition | A larger supply of balanced presentations that resist naive search |

Neither family has settled the conjecture. What they have done is set the
benchmark, and the history matters: a presentation being hard to trivialise is
not evidence that it cannot be, and presentations once treated as likely
counterexamples have later been trivialised by better search.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> That record is the reason this repository reports budgets rather than
> conclusions. The candidate counterexamples have a habit of turning out to be
> candidates only.

## What this repository takes from it

| Taken | From |
| :--- | :--- |
| Point a new search at the Akbulut–Kirby family first, and publish the budget it stopped at | The way that family has been used as a benchmark |
| Treat a failed search as a fact about the search | The presentations that were trivialised after being assumed hard |
| Verify every path independently of whatever found it | That a submitted solution is only a list of moves |
